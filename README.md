## AI + Media

Fewer people, less money, more checking, lower trust - all at once. Two tracks, sixteen 45-minute sessions:

- **Editor track (6)** - what AI changes in a media org, the provenance problem and why detection is not the answer, rights and licensing, newsroom policy and red lines, audience economics after search, and leading the change.
- **Producer track (10)** - one running story at the Harbour Ledger: a port expansion whose press release claims a 40% emissions cut while the filing says 40% per container, on a projection, by 2031. From tip to publication in text, audio and video, provenance intact.

A live verification desk runs on the key pages: toggle the five checks - reverse search, metadata and C2PA, two independent sources, primary document, expert call - and watch one night of inbound climb from 1 out of 10 items handled correctly to 10 out of 10. The sixth toggle is an AI detector. It never raises the score, and at the top of the ladder it flags a genuine wire photograph. Finding out why is the point.

Taught from the Reuters Institute's Journalism, Media and Technology Trends and Predictions 2026 (280 leaders across 51 countries) and Digital News Report 2026, the C2PA provenance standard, and long-standing newsroom verification practice. Rights and licensing content is orientation, not legal advice.
